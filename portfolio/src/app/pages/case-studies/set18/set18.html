Overview

This case study covers my experience working on the BoxBox Bootcamp team for Set 18. Highlights from the development and design process are featured below.

CONTEXT

What is the BoxBox Bootcamp?

Bootcamp is a Riot Games-funded event run by Twitch streamer, BoxBox. It is a competition where streamers and community members try to climb the in-game ranked ladder as quickly as they can before they get eliminated from the event. Bootcamp happens at the start of every set and happens about three times a year.

Why is the Bootcamp website important?

The Bootcamp website is where participants can track how they are doing in the event. It is Bootcamp’s most critical platform as it handles eliminations and shows participant rankings in real time. It also serves as an information hub for participants and viewers alike.

THE PROBLEM

“The leaderboards aren’t interesting enough”

After improving the user experience for the leaderboards last set, the team wanted to expand the leaderboard features. We noted that the leaderboards were less engaging to view from a viewer perspective when compared to the experience of a streamer that was participating and actively checking their position.

Taking on a new role

Due to departures in the Bootcamp team, I was assigned additional admin tasks for this set. This change meant that I had more front-loaded tasks earlier in the Bootcamp timeline, which required more precise time management in order to meet all deliverable requirements in time.

THE PROCESS

Balancing three separate roles...

Our team had about 6 weeks in total to design, build the front-end from scratch, and refactor the back-end API. I worked in parallel with another developer, who handled all the back-end development, databases, and hosting infrastructure.

Design Goals

I iterated over the previous website design used for Set 17. Again, I favored efficiency when updating the design since I had to refactor some front-end code and handle admin responsibilities. I focused on three main areas of improvement/change and completed the design update in a week.

01

Update the website design to match the new set theme

Swapped to forest-themed backgrounds and included new set-specific assets (page furniture and iconography) provided by the branding team at Riot.

Updated typefaces to fit the new set theme (whimsical, forest-like aesthetics). Ensured body font was a serif typeface to ensure better readability.

02

Add new placement and LP change feature to the leaderboards

I brainstormed two new features to add to the leaderboards: badges and placement/elo tracking. Badges would allow participants to choose a flair to display on their scorecard.

The team decided to go with just placement/elo tracking for this set due to time constraints and to also run a pilot first to see if this change would clutter the leaderboard.

03

Design a proper mobile leaderboard interface

Due to time constraints, I couldn't fully prototype a mobile view for the leaderboard. I decided using a modal pop-up would be an easy, intuitive way to fix the mobile issue. Pictured above are my rough sketches for a modal pop-up to add to the leaderboard.

Pictured above is the final modal on the mobile view. I decided to use a vertical orientation to take advantage of the portrait orientation of most mobile devices. I also shuffled a few elements around to make the view more intuitive.

Development

Minimal time was spent on development this set. I made the asset swaps early on in the project timeline and spent time mostly on adding the new placement/LP change tracking feature to the leaderboards. After public launch of the updated website, I updated the mobile leaderboard interface.

Admin/Event Organizer

Since this was my first time working as an admin/event organizer with the Bootcamp team, there were a lot of brand new tasks for me to pick up. I spent a lot of time helping the team screening applications before the event started. I also helped answer tickets throughout the event. This experience gave me far more insight on how Bootcamp is run from an internal admin perspective, which I plan to use to design tools that can streamline the admin experience.
